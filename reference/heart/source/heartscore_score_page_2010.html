The above graph shows that the HEART score is a perfect predictor of MACE within six weeks after presentation. The X axis represents the HEART score a patient scores, while the Y axis indicates the increase in risk of a cardiac event. The S shaped curve represents a discrimination between low and high risk patients. Low HEART scores accompany a low likelihood of an ACS; high HEART scores predict high numbers of MACE.

HEART Score Risk of MACE Proposed Policy
0 - 3 0,9% Discharge
4 - 6 12% X-ECG
7 - 10 65% CAG

Patients can be divided into three distinct groups. A score of 0-3 points carries a risk of 0.9% for reaching a MACE, and therefore supports a policy of early discharge. With this very low risk percentage in mind it is doubtful whether additional diagnostic procedures at the outpatient clinic are useful.

In case of a HEART score of 4-6 points immediate discharge is not an option, as this figure indicates a risk of 12.0% for a MACE within six weeks. Such patients should be admitted for clinical observation, treated as an ACS awaiting final diagnosis, and subjected to non-invasive investigations such as repeated Troponin, exercise testing and possibly advanced ischemia detection. A HEART score ≥ 7 points, with a risk of 65% for a MACE, calls for early aggressive treatments possibly including invasive strategies without preceding non-invasive testing.

HEART Score Criteria

By assigning zero, one, or two points — towards an atypical patient history, ECG anomalies, the patient's age, any risk factors present, and elevated Troponine — patients score on a scale of 0‒10.
