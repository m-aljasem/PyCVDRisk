    TIMI Calculators is a compendium of risk scores developed at the Thrombolysis in Myocardial Infarction (TIMI) Study Group

    Functionalities

    This app can calculate the following TIMI risk scores:

    • TIMI Risk Score for UA/NSTEMI

    • TIMI Risk Score for STEMI

    • TIMI Risk Index for Mortality (STEMI or NSTEMI)

    • TIMI Risk Score for Secondary Prevention (2°P) in ASCVD

    • TIMI Risk Score for Heart Failure in Diabetes (TRS-HFDM)

    • TIMI Biomarker Score for HF in Diabetes

    • TIMI Risk Score for Atherothrombosis in Diabetes

    • TIMI AF score for severe net clinical outcome

    • TIMI Risk Score for Kidney Disease Progression

    • TIMI Risk Score for New Onset of Diabetes

    • TIMI Risk Score SEE in AF

    • Heterogeneity of DOACs vs Warfarin in AF

    Formula, reference and rationale of each score is provided.

    This app has been developed by Giorgio Melloni and Andrea Bellavia and curated by TIMI investigators and clinical fellows. For questions, bug fixes or collaborations, email gmelloni at bwh dot harvard dot edu

    How does it work?

    This score estimates the risk of kidney disease progression (KDP) in patients with type 2 diabetes (T2D).

    Context

    This score was developed as a clinical risk score for the composite kidney endpoint of sustained ≥40% eGFR reduction, end-stage kidney disease (ESKD), or kidney death, in a pooled cohort of 41,195 patients with T2DM from 4 TIMI clinical trial cohorts. Candidate variables were assessed with multivariable Cox regression, and independent variables (p<0.01) retained in the final model

    There was a total of 655 events over a median follow up of 2.7 years. The final model was composed of eight independent predictors: atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease, heart failure, systolic blood pressure, diabetes duration, HbA1c, glomerular filtration rate, urine albumin-creatinine ratio, and hemoglobin

    How does it work?

    This score estimates the 14-day risk of all-cause mortality, new or recurrent myocardial infarction, or severe recurrent ischemia requiring urgent revascularization in patients with unstable angina or NSTEMI.

    Context

    Two phase 3, international, randomized, double-blind trials (the Thrombolysis in Myocardial Infarction 11B trial (TIMI-11B) [August 1996–March 1998] and the Efficacy and Safety of Subcutaneous Enoxaparin in Unstable Angina and Non-Q-Wave MI trial (ESSENCE) [October 1994–May 1996].

    A total of 1957 patients with UA/NSTEMI were assigned to receive unfractionated heparin (test cohort) and 1953 to receive enoxaparin in TIMI 11B; 1564 and 1607 were assigned respectively in ESSENCE.

    The 3 validation cohorts were the unfractionated heparin group from ESSENCE and both enoxaparin groups.

    Formula

    Sum 1 point for each positive risk factor, 0 otherwise

    Limitations

    • This score was developed in cohorts of patients who qualified for enrollment in 2 phase 3 trials of treatment for UA/NSTEMI. Its performance in cohorts of patients who present to emergency departments and physicians’ offices with chest pain must be assessed to determine its generalizability to a variety of clinical settings.
    • Given the quantitative relationship between release of cardiac biomarkers and prognosis, it is possible that the performance of the model could be improved by incorporating a weighting term for small, moderate, and large releases of biomarkers detected at the time of presentation.

    Reference Terms

    • ASA, aspirin
    • CAD, coronary artery disease
    • MI, myocardial infarction
    • NSTEMI, non-ST elevation myocardial infarction
    • UA, unstable angina.

    Notes

    • Risk factors for CAD include family history of CAD, hypertension, hypercholesterolemia, diabetes, or being a current smoker.
    • Chest pain risk scores such as the HEART Score have been shown to better stratify risk than the TIMI Score in the undifferentiated chest pain patient.
    • A TIMI risk score of 0 or 1 does not equal zero risk of adverse outcome. The original study showed 4.7% of patients with a score of 0 or 1 had adverse outcomes within 14 days. Validation studies showed 1.7 to 2.1% of patients with a score of 0 still had adverse outcomes within 30 days.
    • Unclear if this risk score can be used in patients with chest pain in the setting of cocaine use.
    • The TIMI Score was further studied as part of an accelerated diagnostic protocol in the ADAPT trial, which includes estimation of pre-test probability using TIMI, plus abnormal EKG and troponin (high-sensitivity cardiac troponin I).

    Reference

    Antman EM, Cohen M, Bernink PJLM, McCabe CH, Horacek T, Papuchis G, Mautner B, Corbalan R, Radley D, Braunwald E. The TIMI Risk Score for Unstable Angina/Non–ST Elevation MI: A Method for Prognostication and Therapeutic Decision Making. JAMA. 2000;284:835–842.

    How does it work?

    This score estimates the 30-day risk of all-cause mortality in patients with ST Elevation Myocardial Infarction (STEMI). A patient with an ST-Elevation Myocardial Infarction (STEMI) who is post-cath may have bleeding risks and the primary team would like to help objectify risk-benefit of post-PCI medications.

    Context

    This score is the sum of independent predictors of mortality weighted according to the adjusted odds ratios from a logistic regression analysis in the Intravenous nPA for Treatment of Infarcting Myocardium Early II trial (InTIME-II, N=14’114).

    Mean 30-day mortality in the cohort was 6.7%.

    Formula

    Sum the following points if the characteristic is present, 0 otherwise:

    • 2 points if Age 65-74 or 3 points if >= 75
    • 3 points if Blood Pressure < 100
    • 2 points if heart rate > 100
    • 2 points if Killip II-IV
    • 1 point if Anterior ST Elevation or LBBB
    • 1 point if history of diabetes, HTN or angina
    • 1 point if weight < 67 kg
    • 1 point if the time to treatment > 4 hrs

    Limitations

    • The risk score was derived and validated among fibrinolytic-eligible patients enrolled in clinical trials. It is recognized that patients ineligible for thrombolysis or excluded from clinical trials may be at higher risk for adverse outcomes. The absolute quantitative observations made in the present report may not apply to other populations.
    • The TIMI risk score for STEMI is designed for risk assessment early after patient presentation and thus does not incorporate noninvasive and invasive data, including provocative testing for ischemia, evaluation of left ventricular function, and coronary angiography. Furthermore, other important early prognostic indicators, such as cardiac biomarkers and ST-segment resolution, were not included in this analysis.
    • This score was developed based on lytic-treated patients, which likely have worse outcomes when compared to PCI.

    Reference Terms

    • BP, Blood Pressure
    • CHF, Congestive Heart Failure
    • JVD, jugular vein distension
    • LBBB, left bundle branch block
    • HTN, Hypertension

    Notes

    • The score ranges from 0 to 14 points
    • Should be used in patients with diagnosed STEMI, NOT to evaluate patients with chest pain.

    Reference

    Morrow DA, Antman EM, Charlesworth A, Cairns R, Murphy SA, de Lemos JA, Giugliano RP, McCabe CH, Braunwald E. TIMI risk score for ST-elevation myocardial infarction: A convenient, bedside, clinical score for risk assessment at presentation: An intravenous nPA for treatment of infarcting myocardium early II trial substudy. Circulation. 2000;102:2031–2037.

    TIMI Risk Index for Mortality

    STEMI

    How does it work?

    The TIMI Risk Index (TRI) is a simple calculator to predict 30-day mortality, which has been validated in a registry of 150,000 STEMI patients and a registry of over 300,000 NSTEMI patients. It has been shown to both predict in-hospital and long-term mortality.

    Context

    This index was initially derived from 12,353 patients enrolled in the Intravenous nPA for Treatment of Infarcting Myocardium Early (InTIME II-TIMI 17) trial, a randomized trial of lanoteplase versus alteplase as reperfusion therapy for STEMI. A significant graded relationship of the TRI with mortality at 24 h and at 30 days was observed and independently validated in patients with STEMI from the Thrombolysis In Myocardial Infarction (TIMI) 9A and 9B trials. The TRI was validated first in 337,192 patients from the National Registry of Myocardial Infarction with NSTEMI and subsequently with 153,486 patients with STEMI classified by reperfusion status.

    Formula

    TIMI Risk Index = Heart Rate * (Age/10)² / Systolic BP

    Limitations

    • Baseline and treatment differences among patients with STEMI and NSTEMI may exist that were not quantified in this analysis. For example, decisions regarding reperfusion therapy in patients with STEMI were made by treating physicians. This may have affected outcomes for patients and altered the relationships among the groups studied.
    • The risk index provides an initial assessment of risk that should be updated as the patient’s course progresses and more information becomes available (both for STEMI and NSTEMI).

    Reference Terms

    • SBP, systolic blood pressure
    • STEMI, ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction
    • NSTEMI, non–ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction
    • TRI, Thrombolysis In Myocardial Infarction risk index
    • RT, reperfusion therapy

    Notes

    • The score comprises 10 deciles of risk

    Reference

    Wiviott SD, Morrow DA, Frederick PD, Antman EM, Braunwald E; National Registry of Myocardial Infarction. Application of the Thrombolysis in Myocardial Infarction risk index in non-ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction: evaluation of patients in the National Registry of Myocardial Infarction. J Am Coll Cardiol. 2006 Apr 18;47(8):1553-8. Epub 2006 Mar 29. PubMed PMID: 16630990.

    Morrow D, Antman E, Giugliano R, Cairns R, Charlesworth A, Murphy S, de Lemos J, McCabe C, Braunwald E. A simple risk index for rapid initial triage of patients with ST-elevation myocardial infarction: an InTIME II substudy. Lancet. 2001 Volume 358, Issue 9293, 10 November 2001, Pages 1571-1575

    How does it work?

    This score estimates the incidence rates of hospitalization for heart failure in patients with diabetes.

    Context

    This score was developed as a clinical risk score for HHF in 8212 patients with T2DM in the placebo arm of SAVOR-TIMI 53 (Saxagliptin Assessment of Vascular Outcomes Recorded in Patients With Diabetes Mellitus-Thrombolysis in Myocardial Infarction 53). Candidate variables were assessed through Cox proportional hazard model and significant predictors were included in a simple arithmetic sum raging from 0 to 7. Results were externally validated in 8578 patients with T2D in the placebo arm of DECLARE-TIMI 58 (Dapagliflozin Effect on Cardiovascular Events-Thrombolysis in Myocardial Infarction 58).

    Formula

    For albumin-to-creatinin ratio, add 1 point if between 30 and 300 mg/g and 2 points if >300. For all the other characteristics, sum 1 point if present, 0 otherwise.

    Limitations

    • The derivation cohort database did not include details of the HF history or echocardiographic data, including indicators of HHF risk such as left ventricular ejection fraction and diastolic dysfunction. these variables were not included as candidate risk variables in the derivation of this risk score.
    • Creatinine clearance <60 mL/min was an exclusion criterion in the DECLARE-TIMI 58 trial, the range of diabetic renal disease among patients enrolled in that trial was more restricted, and thus, the proportion of patients with higher risk scores was lower in the validation cohort than in the derivation cohort. This explains why the event rate was similar between derivation and validation cohorts despite a 2-year difference in follow-up.
    • The score was derived and validated in 2 clinical trial cohorts, which could influence the generalizability of the results.

    Reference Terms

    • HF, Heart Failure
    • HHF, Hospitalization for Heart Failure (indicates incident disease)
    • CHF, Congestive Heart Failure (indicates prevalent disease)
    • T2D, Type 2 Diabetes

    Notes

    • The score ranges from 0 to 7 points
    • Check out the complementary TIMI Biomarker Score for Heart Failure in Diabetes

    Reference

    Berg DD, Wiviott SD, Scirica BM, Gurmu Y, Mosenzon O, Murphy SA, Bhatt DL, Leiter LA, McGuire DK, Wilding JPH, et al. Heart Failure Risk Stratification and Efficacy of Sodium-Glucose Cotransporter-2 Inhibitors in Patients With Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus. Circulation. 2019;140:1569–1577.

    How does it work?

    This score estimates the incidence rates of hospitalization for heart failure in patients with diabetes using clinically available biomarkers.

    Context

    A risk score for HHF was derived using high-sensitivity troponin T (hsTnT) and N-terminal prohormone of B-type natriuretic peptide (NT-proBNP) from 6,106 placebo-treated patients with T2DM in SAVOR-TIMI 53 (Saxagliptin Assessment of Vascular Outcomes Recorded in Patients with Diabetes Mellitus–Thrombolysis in Myocardial Infarction 53). Candidate variables were assessed using Cox regression. The strongest indicators of HHF risk were included in the score using integer weights. The score was externally validated in 7,251 placebo-treated patients in DECLARE-TIMI 58 (Dapagliflozin Effect on CardiovascuLAR Events–Thrombolysis in Myocardial Infarction 58).

    Median follow-up in the derivation cohort (SAVOR-TIMI 53) was 2 years with 3% event rate and 13% prevalent CHF at baseline

    Median follow-up in the validation cohort (DECLARE-TIMI 58) was 4 years with 3% event rate and 10% prevalent CHF at baseline

    Formula

    Sum points according to the table below

    Risk categories were defined based on the distribution of HHF event rates across integer risk scores in the derivation cohort:

    • low risk (0–3 points)
    • intermediate risk (4–6 points)
    • high risk (7–8 points)
    • very high risk (9–11 points)

    Limitations

    • The score was derived and validated in 2 clinical trial cohorts, which could influence the generalizability of the results.
    • Patients with severe kidney disease (i.e., eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73 m²) were excluded from both trials and were therefore not represented in the derivation or validation cohorts. Since the concentrations of both hsTnT and NT-proBNP are affected by impaired renal clearance, additional validation studies would be needed to assess the performance of the biomarker-based risk score in patients with eGFR <30 mL/min/1.73 m².
    • This derivation of the biomarker-based risk score uses cut points for the high-sensitivity cardiac troponin assay and natriuretic peptide assay used in this study, which cannot automatically be applied to other assays. Future studies are needed to define analogous cut points for BNP and assays for high-sensitivity troponin I (hsTnI).
    • The derivation cohort database did not include echocardiographic data, such as left ventricular ejection fraction, so we did not include left ventricular ejection fraction as a candidate risk indicator and did not separately model risk of HHF with HF with reduced ejection fraction versus with preserved ejection fraction.

    Reference Terms

    • HF, Heart Failure
    • HHF, Hospitalization for Heart Failure (indicates incident disease)
    • CHF, Congestive Heart Failure (indicates prevalent disease)
    • T2D, Type 2 Diabetes
    • NT-proBNP, N-terminal prohormone of B-type natriuretic peptide
    • hsTnT, high-sensitivity troponin T

    Notes

    • The score ranges from 0 to 11 points
    • Check out the complementary TIMI Risk Score for HF in Diabetes

    Reference

    Berg DD, Wiviott SD, Scirica BM, Zelniker TA, Goodrich EL, Jarolim P, Mosenzon O, Cahn A, Bhatt DL, Leiter LA, et al. A Biomarker-Based Score for Risk of Hospitalization for Heart Failure in Patients With Diabetes. Diabetes Care. 2021 Nov;44(11):2573-2581.

    How does it work?

    The choice between initiating a non-vitamin K antagonist oral anticoagulant (NOAC) and a vitamin K antagonist (VKA) in patients with atrial fibrillation (AF) may be challenging. To assist in this decision, we developed a risk score to assess patients’ net clinical outcome by identifying the risk of disabling stroke, life-threatening bleeding, and all-cause mortality at 3 years and identify patients for whom a therapeutic benefit of NOACs over VKA is predicted.

    Context

    ENGAGE AF-TIMI 48 was a randomized clinical trial of edoxaban vs. warfarin in 21,105 patients with AF. Cox proportional hazard models identified factors associated with a serious net clinical outcome (NCO) of disabling stroke, life-threatening bleeding, and all-cause mortality in VKA naı¨ve patients from the warfarin arm. These were used to develop an integer risk score. Performance was assessed by C-indices and validation by bootstrapping

    Sum points according to the table below:

    Risk is calculated based on this table:

    Limitations

    • ENGAGE AF-TIMI 48 was a registration pathway trial and therefore was purposely enriched for higher risk individuals with a CHADS2 score of at least 2. Nearly 22% of patients in the VKA naïve population utilized in this analysis had a CHADS2 score > 3. Therefore, the ability for this risk score to predict a differential therapeutic benefit between a NOAC and VKA in patients with low (0–1 points) CHADS2 scores is unknown
    • Since the TIMI-AF score was derived and internally validated from a single population on VKA, overfitting is a potential limitation
    • This analysis was limited to patients who benefit from either edoxaban or VKA, and therefore application of the TIMI-AF risk score to the other NOACs from trial populations of varying baseline stroke and bleeding risk warrants ongoing study.

    • The score ranges from 0 to 17 points
    • Points over 12 are aggregated

    How does it work?

    This score estimates the risk of Systemic Embolic Event (SEE) in patients with atrial fibrillation (AF), to support clinical risk stratification and decision-making.

    The SEE risk calculator estimates an individual’s risk of SEE over a 2.7-year period using clinical variables independently associated with SEE in patients with AF.

    Context

    SEE are a serious but often under-recognized complication of AF.

    While the benefit of non–vitamin K antagonist oral anticoagulants (NOACs) for stroke prevention is well established, less is known about their effect on SEE and about the characteristics of patients most at risk.

    This risk prediction model was developed from individual patient data from COMBINE-AF, a pooled dataset of the pivotal randomized trials comparing NOACs with warfarin in patients with AF.

    Among 71,683 patients followed, 188 experienced an SEE (annualized rate 0.13% per patient-year). Independent predictors of SEE were identified, and a risk score was derived and validated, showing superior discrimination compared to the CHA₂DS₂-VASc score for SEE prediction.

    Formula

    Sum points according to the table below

    Covariate β Estimate HR (95% CI)
    Creatinine Clearance (per mL/min) –0.02058 0.98 (0.97–0.99)
    Paroxysmal AF (vs persistent) –0.64663 0.52 (0.31–0.89)
    VKA Naïve –0.37262 0.69 (0.47–1.00)
    Female sex 0.68764 1.99 (1.34–2.94)
    History of Stroke/TIA 0.63246 1.88 (1.30–2.73)
    History of MI 0.57852 1.78 (1.14–2.79)
    History of PAD 1.15593 3.18 (1.87–5.39)
    Current Smoker 0.81362 2.26 (1.25–4.06)

    The total score corresponds to a predicted SEE risk over the follow-up period.

    Limitations

    • The model was derived from clinical trial populations, which may not fully represent patients in routine clinical practice.
    • The relatively small number of SEE events (n=188) may limit precision, particularly at score extremes.
    • External validation in real-world, independent cohorts is warranted to assess generalizability.
    • The analysis included warfarin and standard-dose NOACs; applicability to reduced-dose regimens or off-label use is uncertain

    Reference Terms

    • AF: Atrial fibrillation
    • SEE: Systemic embolic event
    • IS: Ischemic stroke
    • NOAC: Non–vitamin K antagonist oral anticoagulant
    • VKA: Vitamin K antagonist
    • TIA: Transient ischemic attack
    • CHA₂DS₂-VASc: Stroke risk score in AF
    • CrCl: Creatinine clearance

    Notes

    • The SEE risk score outperforms CHA₂DS₂-VASc in predicting SEE (C-index: 0.74 vs 0.62).
    • Although SEEs were ~10× less common than ischemic strokes, they were associated with similar mortality and substantial morbidity.
    • This model introduces the first validated, SEE-specific risk prediction tool, supporting improved risk stratification in clinical practice.
