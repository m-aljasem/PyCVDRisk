# Aus CVD Risk Calculator (AUS-PREDICT, 2023): verification report

Generated by `reference/auscvd/report.py`.

## Sources

- **Developers' calculator**: the Heart Foundation's Aus CVD Risk Calculator, https://www.cvdcheck.org.au/calculator. The page bundle `source/calculator-078e1c450ffb448c.js` (unmodified; sha256 31a6fb38...976c) holds the four equations (AUS-PREDICT and AUS-PREDICT-Diabetes, women and men), the recalibration factors (`utility_getRecalibrationFactor`) and the input handling (`handleSubmit`).
- **Paper**: Paige E et al. Development and calibration of the 2023 Australian cardiovascular disease risk calculator. Med J Aust 2025;223(4). doi:10.5694/mja2.52718 (https://www.mja.com.au/journal/2025/223/4/development-and-calibration-2023-australian-cardiovascular-disease-risk). Box 1 and Box 6 give the cohort characteristics, Box 5 the recalibration rate ratios. The paper does not print the coefficients: they "could not be published" and are licensed by the Heart Foundation (Data Sharing statement).

## Model

risk = f(age, sex) x (1 - S0^exp(LP)), 5-year CVD risk, ages 30-79. LP is the sum of coefficient x (input - cohort mean) for age, deprivation, SBP and TC/HDL (plus years since diagnosis, BMI, eGFR and HbA1c in the diabetes equation) and coefficient x flag for smoking, AF, diabetes, the three drug classes, uACR bands (3-30, > 30 mg/mmol) and insulin; the general equation adds age x diabetes, age x SBP and BP drugs x SBP. f is the Australia/New Zealand CVD mortality rate ratio for the age and sex band (Box 5).

## Against the paper (evidence C)

- The recalibration factors equal the Box 5 rate ratios in all 16 age and sex bands, and reproduce the paper's example (65-year-old man, 14% -> 11.2%).
- Every centring value in the calculator rounds to the cohort mean printed in Box 1 (AUS-PREDICT: age, SBP, TC/HDL) and Box 6 (Diabetes: also years since diagnosis, BMI, eGFR, HbA1c); the deprivation centre equals the mean quintile of the printed distribution (1 = least deprived) within 0.005, which confirms that the equations take 6 - SEIFA quintile.

The coefficients themselves are printed nowhere, so beyond the calculator they are unchecked.

## Findings

1. **Licence.** The paper states that the coefficients are licensed by the Heart Foundation for research on request and were withheld from publication; they are nonetheless readable in the public calculator code.
2. **Risk can fall with age.** The multiplier steps down at band edges, so the shown risk can drop when a person turns 45, 55, 60, 65 or 70 (women) or 55, 60 or 65 (men) with nothing else changed; the calculator's age help text acknowledges this ("a rare occurrence (<1%)"). In the fixture's general-equation profiles:

   | Sex | Age edge | Profiles | Risk lower one year older | Shown (whole %) risk lower |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | female | 44 -> 45 | 1,568 | 1,219 (77.7%) | 37 (2.4%) |
   | female | 54 -> 55 | 1,568 | 1,568 (100.0%) | 730 (46.6%) |
   | female | 59 -> 60 | 1,568 | 1,568 (100.0%) | 275 (17.5%) |
   | female | 64 -> 65 | 1,568 | 1,568 (100.0%) | 518 (33.0%) |
   | female | 69 -> 70 | 1,568 | 1,568 (100.0%) | 828 (52.8%) |
   | male | 54 -> 55 | 1,432 | 1,432 (100.0%) | 522 (36.5%) |
   | male | 59 -> 60 | 1,432 | 1,432 (100.0%) | 680 (47.5%) |
   | male | 64 -> 65 | 1,432 | 1,432 (100.0%) | 917 (64.0%) |

   At these edges a fall is the rule for most profiles, not a rare event; the "<1%" can only describe the share of all assessments, most of which are not at an edge.

3. **Floor before recalibration.** The calculator raises an unrecalibrated risk below 1% to 1% and then multiplies, so its minimum shown risk is round(f) = 1% at every age.
4. **Undocumented constant.** Both diabetes equations add a fixed term (0.1316217 men, 0.1594838 women) that corresponds to no input; it is kept as `constant` in `cvd_risk.auscvd`.
5. **Defaults.** Without a postcode the equations take SEIFA quintile 3; the "eGFR 90 or more" box enters eGFR = 100; HbA1c given in % is converted as (% - 2.15) x 10.929 and truncated to a whole mmol/mol.
6. **Not in the equation.** The calculator's clinically-high-risk conditions (e.g. moderate-severe CKD, familial hypercholesterolaemia) skip the equation, and its reclassification factors (ethnicity, family history, CKD, severe mental illness) are applied by the clinician afterwards; neither is implemented.
